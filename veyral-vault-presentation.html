Veyral · the document hoard

Your documents. Sealed. Only what you may see comes back.

Veyral Vault stores, organises, versions and shares a company's files — and its own AI librarian answers from the files a requester may see, with sources.

The problem

Documents leak. Access rules get lost. AI sees everything.

Most systems hand the model the whole archive and hope the prompt is enough. It is not. The vault decides, in code, what each person, product or agent may read — and gives the model only that.

The answer

One sentence.

"The vault decides, in code, what each person, product or agent may read, and gives the model only that — with three levels per document: nothing, cleaned, or full."

Everything else is a detail of that sentence, or a later phase.

The core idea

Three levels on every document.

None
Not listed. Never used in an answer.
View-only
Watermarked preview. No download. The librarian may answer from it.
Full
Open, download, answer.

A fourth level — cleaned — exists where the cleaning extra is on: names, amounts and IDs removed from answers and copies.

How it works

Four steps. Every request. Every door.

  • Who is asking — a person, a product or an agent, with a level.
  • What they may see — decided in code, before the model.
  • The model gets only that — chunks, never the archive.
  • The answer, with sources — and an audit line, without storing the answer.

The same pipeline for the UI, the MCP door and the REST API. No second path.

The judge

JEV — local AI that checks the answer.

A set of local models that route the question, rerank the search, ground every sentence in its source, and screen for hidden instructions. Without it, the vault falls back to word-match and says so.

What makes it different

Access in code, not in prompts.

  • The policy filter runs before retrieval. The librarian never receives a chunk the requester may not see.
  • Levels are relations, not flags. reader_full ⊃ reader_redacted ⊃ nothing.
  • Every access is recorded. The answer itself is not stored — a hash and the metadata.
  • Cleaning is pattern- and model-based, and will miss entities. It reduces risk; it does not eliminate it.
Your keys, your rules

A vault is one client's sealed store.

  • One directory per vault. Copied and raised again on another server. No shared database, no shared key.
  • Nothing binds publicly. Every service binds to localhost; a name is published by the host's proxy.
  • Append, never rewrite. A corrected paper keeps its old text under a dated heading.
  • Your keys, your host, your model — or ours, declared and accepted per client.
